Cloud Options (Free Tiers)

Free AI Transcription and Captions Guide · Asdale Dynamics, LLC

What "Cloud" Means

Your audio file is uploaded to a company's server. Their AI transcribes it. The text comes back to you.

Pros: No install required · No powerful computer needed · Usually higher accuracy · Speaker labels often included

Cons: Your audio leaves your computer (privacy — see Chapter 11) · Free tiers have limits · Needs internet

Option A: YouTube Auto-Captions (Best for Videos)

Cost: Free  |  Privacy: Video is public unless set to "unlisted"

  1. Go to studio.youtube.com and sign in
  2. Click Create → Upload videos
  3. Upload your video
  4. In visibility, choose Unlisted (not public)
  5. Wait for processing (YouTube generates auto-captions)
  6. Go to Subtitles in the left sidebar
  7. Click your video, then English (Automatic)
  8. Review and edit the auto-generated captions
  9. Click Actions → Download → .vtt or .srt

Option B: Google Docs Voice Typing (Best for Meetings & Dictation)

Cost: Free  |  Privacy: Audio goes to Google; stored in your Google Doc

  1. Open docs.google.com in Chrome or Edge
  2. Create a new document
  3. Click Tools → Voice typing
  4. Click the microphone icon that appears
  5. Speak clearly — text appears in real time
  6. Click the microphone again to stop
  7. Save or download the document

Option C: AssemblyAI (Best for Speaker Labels)

Cost: Free tier ~5 hours/month  |  Privacy: Upload to their servers

  1. Go to assemblyai.com and sign up
  2. Get your free API key from the dashboard
  3. Use their Playground to upload audio without coding
  4. Enable Speaker Diarization for speaker labels
  5. Download the transcript as SRT, VTT, or TXT

Option D: Otter.ai (Best for Meeting Notes)

Cost: 600 minutes/month free  |  Privacy: Upload to their servers

  1. Go to otter.ai and sign up
  2. Upload an audio file or record live
  3. Otter transcribes and identifies speakers
  4. Edit the transcript in their web editor
  5. Export as TXT, PDF, SRT, or DOCX

Privacy Warning for Cloud Options

When you use cloud transcription:

  • Your audio is sent to a third-party server
  • It may be stored temporarily or longer
  • Free tier terms can change

Do not use cloud tools for: patient health information, confidential business meetings, personal/financial data, anything you wouldn't upload to email.

Use local tools (Chapter 4) for sensitive audio.

Try It: Transcribe a Short Clip

  1. Record a 30-second voice memo on your phone
  2. Upload to YouTube Studio as unlisted
  3. Wait ~5 minutes for auto-captions
  4. Download the .vtt file
  5. Open it in a text editor — you now have a transcript

Key Takeaways

  • Cloud = easy but not private — your audio leaves your device
  • YouTube auto-captions are free and good for videos
  • Google Docs Voice Typing is great for real-time dictation
  • AssemblyAI and Otter give speaker labels but need accounts
  • Free tiers have limits — track your monthly minutes
